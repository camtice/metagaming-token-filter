Frontrunner Sets

The best-performing SAE feature groups, judged on the two metrics that matter: generalization to new documents of familiar kinds, and generalization to a domain the selection never saw. Candidates use the recommended recipe (ratio screen · vote k=4 · windowing); the paper rule, hill-climb, and control-cap rows are kept as references.

F2 train
Score on G-TRAIN — the dense c1/c3/c4 halves + control half the selection statistics were computed from. Not a result; the anchor for measuring overfit.
F2 in-distribution
Held-out documents of trained-on kinds: human rollout spans + the unseen halves of the same dense classes, precision vs held-out controls. "Does it work on new data?"
F2 held-out domain
The same, plus all of dense c2 (AI-safety) — a category with zero training exposure — counted as forget. "Does it work on new kinds of data?" The headline.

Reading it

1. The candidates hold .76–.82 held-out-domain F2 at 1–5% control FP — and their Δ column is positive throughout: the ratio screen's sets get better when the unseen domain enters the metric, because topical selection transfers. 2. Both reference pathologies are visible in the slope chart: hill-climb's train score evaporates on held-out data, and the control-cap's line drops again at the third station (its features are domain-arbitrary). 3. Pool choice (fable vs haiku-v8) moves the numbers ~.02–.04 with the same recipe; width moves them ~.02–.03 in favor of 65k. The recipe, not the pool, is the decision.

Before freezing a set: external validity (westover / kimi AUC) has been measured on close siblings of these cells but not on these exact v8 cells, and the sealed VAL-A/VAL-B spend remains unspent — both should gate the final choice.
